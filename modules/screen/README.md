# screen

The venue screens ([contracts/screen.md](../../contracts/screen.md)). It holds the module's skeleton, devices with pairing, zones with assignments and mirroring (#220), device heartbeat and health (#222), playlists, schedule windows, pushes and the tick (#216), the asset library (#217), the ticker and photos (#218), moving devices between events (#221), the composed state (#109), the push channel (#304), the player's core (#305), its rendering (#306), its content widgets (#308), and offline, the virtual screen and the kiosk check (#309); the rest of the player (#307, #310) builds on it.

- **Stack:** Node 22, Fastify 5, `pg`, on `@canccu/kit` ([0006](../../docs/decisions/0006-stack.md)). Port 8080. Caddy routes `/api/screen/*`, `/screen/*` and `/admin/screen/*` here unchanged, `/screen` to `/screen/`, `/admin/screen` to `/admin/screen/` and `/api/screen/health` to `/health`.
- **Env:** the kit's (`CANCCU_MODULE`, `CANCCU_VERSION`, `CANCCU_VALKEY_URL`, `CANCCU_ADMIN_URL`, `CANCCU_SERVICE_KEY`), plus `CANCCU_DATABASE_URL` (the login role it serves as), `CANCCU_MIGRATE_DATABASE_URL` (the migration role), `CANCCU_TENANTS_URL`, `CANCCU_SIGNIN_HOSTNAME`, `CANCCU_SUPERADMIN_HOSTNAME`, `CANCCU_IP_MODE` and, optionally, `CANCCU_FFPROBE` (the ffprobe binary; default `ffprobe` on the PATH), `CANCCU_AGENDA_URL`, `CANCCU_THEMING_URL` and `CANCCU_NOTIFY_URL` (where the composed state reads the run-of-show, the theme and the check-in code; default `http://agenda:8080`, `http://theming:8080` and `http://notify:8080`).
- **Data:** `migrations/*.sql`, run at start by the kit's `runMigrations` (ledger `screen_migrations`). `devices` is the one global table (device identity only); every other table (`screen_assignments`, `screen_zones`, `screen_zone_seeds`, `screen_playlists` with `screen_playlist_items`, `screen_schedules`, `screen_pushes`, `screen_zone_playback`, `screen_agenda_items`, `screen_assets`, `screen_asset_versions`, `screen_files`, `screen_ticker`, `screen_ticker_windows`, `screen_photos`, `screen_render_jobs`) is per event under RLS, and requests run as `canccu_screen_app`. Open pairing codes live in Valkey for 10 minutes, transfer codes and device commands for 60 seconds, device health (the last heartbeat, online deadlines and page loads) for up to 7 days; no device token is ever stored, only its SHA-256. Migration 007 renames `assignments` to `screen_assignments`, and the screen code before it names only the old table: to roll the image back past 007, first undo it by hand (each of its `RENAME … TO` reversed: the table, its constraints and indexes) and delete its row from `screen_migrations`, so the next deploy runs it again.
- **Scheduler core** (`src/schedule/`, pure, no routes yet): weekly windows in the event's time zone (`window.ts`), the winner among a zone's weekly and agenda-bound windows (`winner.ts`) and "what would play at…" with the next change (`simulate.ts`), and the calendar of a zone between two times (`calendar.ts`: each window's runs, the segments cut wherever the active runs change, each with the scheduler's winner and the rule every loser lost on, and the break playlists' daily spans; one read of the local clock per real minute, so the skipped and the repeated hour come out right). Clock, time zone, the agenda items' times and the playlists' priorities come in as arguments, so the tick and the simulate route (#216) share one rule and the daylight-saving tests pin every input. Ported from the legacy signage `scheduler.ts` and `schedule-window.ts` with their tests; the calendar's pointer maths are Studio's (`src/studio/calendar-pointer.ts`).
- **Playback core** (`src/playback/`, pure, no routes yet): a playlist's timeline and frames with their progress-bar timing (`timeline.ts`, `engine.ts`); bookmarks that the tick pauses when a playlist stops and resumes when it plays again, so a deck comes back at its slide and no offset counts twice; a zone's `break` playlists as one state value the tick advances with `stepBreaks` (`breaks.ts`: due times, the daily window, priority chaining, the `break_min_gap_seconds` gap) and what a running break shows (`break-play.ts`: play-once, a looping timeline that goes on where the last break stopped, slideshow dwell with up to 10 s overrun, a shuffle seeded by zone, playlist and start). `projectBreaks` runs that step forward on a copy for simulate's `in_break` and `next_change_at`. Ported from the legacy signage `playback-engine.ts`, `playback-bookmark.ts`, `advt-scheduler.ts`, `advt-window.ts`, `advt-gap-settings.ts` and `advt-playback.ts` with their tests.
- **Content** (`src/content/`): playlists (one document with items, break settings and `revision`), schedule windows (weekly or bound to a run-of-show item, `revision` for conflicts), pushes and simulate (the windows and breaks at a time, not pushes, which play only now; breaks are projected about two weeks ahead at most), every write audited in its transaction. Bodies are checked in `input.ts` (ported from the legacy `api-schemas.ts` and the length and slide-range rules of `playlist-builder.ts`), and against the assets in `asset-rules.ts`: items and pushes name this event's assets, slide fields go only on decks, an automatic length only on video (a slideshow's images take its dwell), and a range ends within the rendered deck. A deck's ranges are clamped to it when a replace's render succeeds or a revert brings back a rendered version, and the playlists that change get a new `revision`. `agenda.ts` keeps `screen_agenda_items` from the `agenda.item.*` events, read from the streams' start at every boot, so bound windows follow delays, crew items included; it keeps each item's title and visibility for the calendar (migration 010), and a replay re-applies an item's last event, so rows kept before that fill at the next boot.
- **The tick** (`src/tick/`): every second, in every container, each zone of each event with screens advances once (`advanceZone`: the newest push above the winning window, bookmarks paused around breaks and switches, `stepBreaks`); a Valkey claim per zone and second gives each step to one container, and only a changed state is written to `screen_zone_playback`. It also ends expired pushes (`screen.content.cleared`, `reason: expired`, audited without an actor). Reads, simulate included, never write. Assets come through an `AssetReader`; until #217 supplies one nothing is playable, so breaks are skipped and bookmarks stay at the start.
- **Assets** (`src/assets/`): uploads are checked by content, never by name (`sniff.ts`); images are turned upright, fitted within 2560×1440 and re-encoded without metadata (`image.ts`, sharp); video and sound are probed with `ffprobe` (`probe.ts`), and anything the screens do not play is 400 `media_unsupported` with a message naming what was found and what to export. Files are content-addressed under `/files/<tenant>/screen/files/<sha256>.<ext>` (`files.ts`): written to `tmp/`, renamed into place, reserved against the event's storage quota at tenants first (the kit's `tenantsStorage`), shared by identical bytes, and deleted with the last row that refers to them, under a per-file advisory lock so a delete and an upload of the same bytes never cross. Once an hour per event, in one container (a Valkey claim), the storage upkeep (`upkeep.ts`) removes stored files without a row and the scratch (`tmp/render-<job>-<attempt>`) of render attempts no longer running, then sets screen's count at tenants to what its files, renders and running attempts take (the repair path); a writer holds a shared per-event lock from its reserve to its commit, so the recount never loses an upload under way. A replace keeps one undo version; version numbers are never reused. Web assets need an origin on `web_allowlist`, YouTube links become youtube-nocookie embeds with their oEmbed health; both need the `screen.web` flag. `GET /api/screen/files/{file}` serves the event's devices and `screen.content.read` sessions, with byte ranges. Ported from the legacy signage `asset-upload.ts`, `image-resize.ts`, `image-orientation.ts`, `video-duration.ts` (now ffprobe), `asset-replace.ts`, `asset-catalog.ts`, `web-asset.ts`, `youtube-asset.ts` and `youtube-oembed.ts` with their tests. Playlist items and active pushes keep an asset in use (`used_by` names them; a delete answers `asset_in_use`), and `readAssets` (`reader.ts`) gives the tick and simulate the assets' kind, length, slide count and health.
- **Pins** (`src/pins/`, #265; contracts/screen.md, Pins and Timed pins and freeze): one row per event (`screen_pins`) with the values, `until`, the `restores` a timed pin puts back, and who changed it (the name kept for Studio's banner). `PUT` takes the values (checked against the kept bundles and theming's revisions), the freeze form (what is in effect, read by the server in the same request) or `freeze: false`; each change is audited, announced as `screen.pin.changed` and queued as a `reload` for every screen of the event, and the composed state draws the pinned revision (`look.ts`; the current one when the pinned read fails) and names the pinned bundle. The tick (`tick.ts`) restores a pin at `until`, or clears it when what it restores is gone; events come from the global `screen_pin_tenants` (a plain tenant reference), so an event with no screen is restored on time too. The bundle store (`bundles.ts`) is `/files/_platform/screen/bundles/<version>/`: each container copies its version in at start (aside, then renamed into place) and then removes all but the newest 5, its own and every version a pin names or restores (`keepBundles`). Theming keeps the revisions a pin names or restores from the same event (modules/theming, `pins.ts`).
- **Ticker** (`src/ticker/`): a person's line (`PUT`, with an end or none) wins over the weekly ticker windows; among windows the one that began last shows, until its occurrence ends in the event's time zone (`resolve.ts`, pure, ported from the legacy `ticker-store.ts` without its read that deleted). Line and windows are kept in Postgres, so a restart loses neither. Every change of what the ticker shows is announced once as `screen.ticker.changed`: by the request that made it, or by the tick when a window starts or ends or a line runs out; the event's ticker row, locked by each, keeps two containers from announcing the same change. Window adds and deletes are audited. The pizza fallback line (`source: pizza`) is not built yet.
- **Photos** (`src/photos.ts`, flag `screen.photos`): images only, normalised as image assets are and stored as the event's content-addressed files, so a photo and an asset with the same bytes share one file and one quota count; listed newest first, `featured` until the player features it. A source's message (`source_ref`) is added once (409 `photo_exists`). `deleteUserPhotos` removes a deleted user's photos in one event. Ported from the API part of the legacy `carousel/photos.ts` with its tests.
- **Render jobs** (`src/render/`, #224, [plan](../../docs/plans/issue-184.md) slice B): a `pptx` or `pdf` upload or replace submits a job (`screen_render_jobs`, the truth), and `POST /api/screen/render-jobs` submits one again; a version's live job, or its render, answers a second submit. The fair queue (`queue.ts`) keeps each event's waiting jobs in a Valkey list and the events with any in a ring; the one renderer slot goes to the event at the head, which then moves to the back, so an event waits behind at most one job of each other event (Lua scripts, so a take is one step). A dispatcher in every container (`dispatch.ts`) takes the next job, streams its deck to `screen-render` over the `render-socket` volume (`client.ts`, which trusts nothing the renderer answers), reserves each slide's bytes against the quota before writing it, and renames the slides into `/files/<tenant>/screen/renders/<asset id>/<asset version>/` when the job is recorded as succeeded; anything else gives the bytes back. A job holds a 30-second lease, renewed every 5 seconds: a cancel is seen at the next renewal and hangs up on the renderer, and a lease that runs out (a lost container) puts the job back at the head of its event's list once, then fails it `renderer_failed`. A render goes with its asset version (a replace keeps the old one for the undo). The queue is rebuilt from Postgres per event, at most once a minute, when its jobs are read. `GET /api/screen/renders/…` serves the slides from the job row, never from a path in the request. The `render` health check is `degraded` while a deck has waited over 10 minutes. Threat model, a compromised renderer: it can only answer, and every answer is checked; one gap is known and low in impact: the socket's `lstat` then `connect` is check-then-use, so the renderer, the only writer of its volume, could swap a link in between, which reaches only Unix sockets in screen's own filesystem (never a file) and is sent only the deck. `CANCCU_RENDER_SOCKET` overrides the socket path (default `/run/canccu-render/render.sock`).
- **Settings:** declared in `modules/admin/declarations/screen.yaml`, read through the kit's settings client. `offline_after_seconds` counts as at least twice `heartbeat_seconds`, so one late heartbeat never takes a screen offline; while admin is down the defaults hold.

`takeCommands` (`src/assignments.ts`) is what the push channel delivers as `command` messages, and `ZONE_USES` (`src/zones.ts`) lists what keeps a zone in use (screens, windows, pushes, break playlists).
- **Composed state** (`src/compose/`): `build.ts` is the pure builder (layout choice, region roles, rotation on the epoch-anchored server clock, idle rules), `parts.ts` the zone's `header` and `hub_qr` options, which draw a header region's widgets, or the hub QR beside its region's other widgets, as parts side by side (each a region of its own in `regions`; the layout itself is unchanged), `widgets.ts` the view-models, `sources.ts` the reads of the other modules: the theme, now and next and the venue info as module reads under the service identity (`GET /internal/theming/current`, `GET /internal/agenda/now`, `GET /internal/tenants/venue-info`), which answer what a guest sees in every info-page mode, and the event's name as a guest read with the event's hostname (a source that is down leaves its widgets `empty` and `stale`), `display.ts` the zone's `display` (the theme's `motion` token, lowered to `calm` while a public `quiet_hours` item of the zone runs, which the agenda's zone read lists among `current`; `drift` from the `burn_in_drift` setting, part of the fingerprint since nothing else carries it), `state.ts` a zone's composition (both orientations from one read of the sources) and the hub's view, and the content widgets (#308), whose sources are read only for a zone that shows them, so their own changes never move another zone's `state_version` (`content.ts`): `playlist` from the tick's stored state (`playing.ts`: the frame the push, the winning window or a running break plays, a `full` break inside the widget too since the state has no place for it yet, with `next_item`; `started_at` is when the frame began, or would have begun had it played from its start, so the view is the same at every instant of one frame and `offset_seconds` is 0), `photo` from the carousel (`photos.ts`: each new photo featured for one `photo_dwell_seconds` after the one before it, then all in turn newest first on the epoch-anchored dwell; the photo list's `featured` follows the same rule), `checkin_qr` from notify's `GET /internal/notify/check-in/code` (purpose `arrival`; a path-only URL made whole on the event's origin; a failed read is `empty` and `stale`, never an old code), and `embed` from the theme's `embeds` by the entry's `embed` name. Each build also says when a content widget next changes by itself (a frame ends, the photo turns, the code rotates), and the hub rebuilds the zone then, in one container (a claim on the instant), so the change reaches screens as a `widget` message; a container that lost the claim looks a second later whether the winner built (its `planned` marker) and plans the next change too, or builds itself, and a failed build is tried again a second later. The tick tells every container which zones' playback it stored (a push, a window or a break switching), over a fan-out channel, and each rebuilds the ones its streams show, so the change does not wait for the old frame's end.
- **Claims** (`src/compose/claims.ts`, `overrides.ts`; #307): Moments' `moments.override.*` events are kept in `screen_claims`, read from the moments streams' start at every boot and applied only when newer than the override's last event (event ids sort by time), so a repeat or a late event changes nothing and an ended override stays ended; an end that lists zones leaves only those. `claims.ts` checks an override whole (a malformed one is dropped), resolves its `expires_at` from `ttl_seconds`, and places a zone's active overrides by the contract's rule: by rank, then the later start, then the id; the first free role of the chain (`regions`, else from `kind`), never `header` or `backdrop`, drawn in the first region of the role; `full` only when no higher claim holds anything but `overlay`; what ranks below a placed `full` is `hidden`, what finds no role `unplaced`. The active overrides are part of the zone's fingerprint, so an expiry or a renewal moves `state_version` within the 10 s zone refresh (the screens end a claim on their own clock anyway). The container whose state advance wins emits `screen.claims.changed` when the landscape placement changed (the event has no orientation). The heartbeat's `claims_sounded` is validated and dropped, and `sound.sounded_claims` stays empty until Moments (step 4); claim sound is not played yet.
- **Push channel** (`src/push/`, [plan](../../docs/plans/issue-109.md) S1–S3): every read of a zone's state (`GET /api/screen/device/state`, a stream's connect, a rebuild) goes through `refreshZone` (`device-state.ts`), which composes the zone and, when the fingerprint of its inputs changed, stores the next `state_version` in Valkey (`store.ts`) in one Lua step that also logs the change (a Valkey stream per zone, trimmed to 5 minutes) and publishes it on the zone's pub/sub channel; the step refuses when the version moved on meanwhile, so of two containers building at once one wins and the other builds again on top of it (up to three tries), since the winner may have read older inputs. `diff.ts` turns two states into the smallest messages per orientation (`theme`, `ticker`, `region`, `widget`; `null` where only a whole `state` will do; an orientation the change left as it was gets a `ticker`, so its screens learn the version). `stream.ts` is one open stream: the first `state` or the resumed changes (`Last-Event-ID` with the zone's key and epoch, and the log still holding every change since), the zone's changes in order (a gap is filled from the log), `command` messages (woken by a nudge on the device's channel, which the commands route publishes), and a `ping` every 15 seconds that resolves the event and checks the device again (a revoked, moved or refused screen loses its stream; a new status, time zone or device name, which a rename announces nowhere else, sends a fresh `state`), counts it as seen, notices a new orientation and catches up on anything a lost pub/sub message left out. `hub.ts` holds a container's streams and reads the screen, agenda, tenants and theming tenant streams: a `tenants` event first clears the kit's cached context of its event (that cache otherwise hears only the platform stream, for up to 30 seconds), and a change of the event's fields, status, venue or venue IP check makes every stream check its device again at once; a device's leaving ends its streams, an assignment re-places them (and every mirror), and the events that change what a zone draws rebuild the zones its streams show, coalesced for 100 ms; every 10 seconds each such zone is also rebuilt in one container (a Valkey claim), for schedule windows and settings, which change with no event. `scripts/demo-stack-test` times the whole path on the demo stack (p95 under 1 s; `test/stack/push-latency.mjs`).
- **Player** (`player/`, [plan](../../docs/plans/issue-109.md) S4, S8): plain TypeScript and DOM, bundled by `npm run build` with esbuild into `dist/player/player.js` and `player.css`. `/screen/` (`src/player.ts`) is the loader: no-store HTML under the kit's event page CSP that names this container's version, names the event's pinned bundle while the store keeps it, else this container's version; `/screen/b/<version>/` serves a version's files as immutable (a version not kept is 404). The page reads `device/state` and turns each answer into a frame (`player/src/player.ts`, `frames.ts`; mockup 05): 401 pairs (the code and the event's name from `GET /api/tenants/current`), a zone plays, `zone: null` is not assigned, 403 is refused, an archived event is the archived notice, 409 `device_moved` follows `device/home` (a `#transfer=` code is redeemed only without a working cookie), anything else (another 403 too) retries behind a connecting frame, which is also the first thing drawn. While playing, `stream.ts` reads the push channel over `fetch` (EventSource hides the status the frames need): resume with `Last-Event-ID`, backoff with jitter up to 30 s, a reconnect after 45 s of silence, and the state polled every 10 s after three failed connects; `<body>` gets `canccu-is-offline` and the "offline since" mark 10 s without a stream. `heartbeat.ts` beats every 15 s and keeps the clock offset. Every answer body is read or cancelled, so a page that runs for days holds no connections, and nothing waits for good: a call gives up after 10 s, a stream connect without headers after 15 s, and a beat is skipped while the last is still open. `screen.ts` draws a zone (plan S5–S7, S11; `player/src/screen.css`, each widget's look in `widgets.css` and `rail.css`, in `@layer canccu-base` so a theme's CSS overrides it): the layout's grid letterboxed to its `aspect`, each region in its cells at its layer, and in it the widget its rotation shows now or its `idle` choice (`logo`, `blank`, `collapse`, `next`; an unknown one draws the logo). Rotation runs on the server's clock (`rotation.ts`: the epoch-anchored cycle and the seeded shuffle of `src/compose/build.ts`, which `test/player-rotation.test.ts` holds it to, since the push leaves cycles and shuffles out), every whole second; the clock's fields and every empty `<time datetime>` (the countdown's) tick on the device (`widget.ts`). A hero's countdown takes its item's start, place and delay from the run of show's row of the same item, and the rail leaves that item out, giving a countdown of it the rail's run of show instead, and the region's idle choice when that leaves no row (`hero-rail.ts`). Widgets draw from the template tree (`tree.ts`: `createElement` and `textContent`, the contract's elements and attributes only, `img` only from this host or `data:`), with the entry's theme template, else the theme's template named as the type, else the player's own (`templates.ts`, one per widget and claim template). A new widget waits up to 2 s for its images, then fades in over the last (`moderate` 600 ms with a small rise, `calm` 1.5 s, `none` at once; `prefers-reduced-motion` makes it `none`), and the ticker fades between texts; new data for what is shown redraws it in place. Burn-in drift sets `--canccu-drift-x`/`-y` each minute (`display.ts`). Text is in `--canccu-u`, 1 % of the layout's short side measured on the layout (a theme's own containers cannot shrink it), at least 2.6 (28 px on a 1080p short side), so 1080p and 4K, landscape and portrait read alike (`test/player-readable.test.ts`). The DOM is kept while the layout stays, so a push never restarts a fade. The `playlist` widget is drawn by the player itself (`media.ts`; a template holds no `video` or `iframe`): images and slides from this host, video from its place on the server's clock (muted unless the screen is the zone's sound device and the item has sound), web pages framed `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin allow-presentation"` without a referrer and hidden when they do not load within 8 s, YouTube embeds started at their place and muted unless they may sound; when the item ends the screen goes on to `next_item` by itself, and a new frame or a new photo fades in like a new widget. The page's CSP adds the event's `web_allowlist` origins and `https://www.youtube-nocookie.com` to `frame-src`. The `embed` widget is drawn by `@canccu/ui`'s `embedWidget()` (a dev dependency: esbuild bundles it). A region with a claim draws it instead (`claims.ts`): its template (`pizza_placed` as `food_status`) with its content, or its `steps` in turn from `starts_at` with the last kept, until its `expires_at` or `ends_at`, on the device's corrected clock, also offline; a takeover hides every region but `_full` and `_overlay` (`data-takeover`), a `hidden` claim shows once the takeover has ended, the overlay draws its claim as a lower-third band, and a claim that ends with `on_end: next` skips the rest of the widget the rotation would show then. The player's tests (`player/test/`) run in happy-dom.
- **A zone for a session** (`src/push/session.ts`, `zone-routes.ts`; contracts/screen.md, Preview and A zone for a session): `GET /api/screen/zones/{zone_key}/state` and `…/stream` (`screen.devices.read`) serve a signed-in person the zone's state and push messages as a mirror's (`device.id` `mirror`, no sound, no commands, no heartbeat); `POST …/preview` (`screen.zones.write`) composes a zone body without saving it (`device.id` `preview`, `state_version` 0, `zone.epoch` `preview`; 400 `unknown_layout` for a layout neither built in nor the theme's). Both streams share `feed.ts` (the first state or the resume, the zone's changes in order, the catch-up); a person's stream checks the session, the right and the admin address rule at every `ping`. The `checkin_qr` view loses its code everywhere a person sees it (its `url`, `image_url`, `rotates_at`, `valid_until` and `qr` are null): in the state, in `region` messages, and in `widget` messages by the types the stream last sent, which is also why such a stream resumes only over changes that keep every region's widgets. Limits per session, across containers: 20 open streams (a Valkey set whose members expire unless pinged) and 30 previews a minute.
- **Offline, the virtual screen and the kiosk check** ([plan](../../docs/plans/issue-109.md) S9, S14; [kiosk guide](../../docs/screen-kiosk.md)): `npm run build` also bundles `player/sw/sw.ts` into `dist/player/sw.js`, served at `/screen/sw.js` (no-cache) and registered by the kiosk page with its bundle version (`?v=`, scope `/screen/`). The worker (`player/src/sw-core.ts`) reads the loader network-first (the cached copy after 5 s or on a failure or 5xx), stores what it reads in the background (`waitUntil`, so a write that fails, such as a full disk, costs only the copy, never the answer), serves immutable addresses (`cacheable.ts`: the bundle, `/theming/a/`, a revision's `/theming/sandbox/<n>/`, `/api/screen/files/`, `/api/screen/renders/`) cache-first, with range reads from the cached file, and keeps exactly the files the page names: those of its current state and `prefetch`, and the fonts and images their CSS names, fetched ahead, the rest evicted. The page (`offline.ts`) keeps the last state in Cache Storage and when it last heard the server; a boot that gets no answer, or one that is not about the device, draws that state and marks it offline since then, while 401, 403 and 409 still go to their frames. Offline, the widgets whose content the server keeps dim (`canccu-is-stale`), while the clock, countdowns and the kept playlist run on at full strength; a check-in code past its `valid_until` (else its `rotates_at`) shows the widget's empty state, also before the 10 s mark (`widget.ts`, `shownView`). The offline mark sits at the top centre, between the header's logo and clock, in `@layer canccu-base`, so a theme restyles it without `!important`. Neither exists on plain http, where the screen behaves as before. `/screen/zones/{zone_key}` (`virtual.ts`) draws a zone through the session paths, with no heartbeat and no offline store (a 404, a zone deleted while it is open, ends the stream with "No such zone" rather than a retry), and draws instead a `ScreenState` its same-origin framing page posts (`{"type": "canccu-screen-preview", "state"}`), for Studio's preview. `/screen/check` (`check.ts`) shows the viewport in device pixels against 1080p, the clock offset against the server (2 s, as `clock_drift`), the device's answer, whether sound plays without a tap (`audio.ts`, which also feeds the heartbeat's `audio_unlocked` and unlocks on the kiosk's first tap) and whether the offline worker runs.
- **Health** (`src/health.ts`): every heartbeat moves the device's deadline (the heartbeat plus `offline_after_seconds`) in one Valkey set per event; a sweep in every container, every 5 seconds, takes the passed deadlines and emits `screen.device.offline` once each. An open push stream counts as seen: on connect and at every `ping` it moves the deadline as a heartbeat does (`recordSeen`, kept apart from the heartbeat's record so neither overwrites the other). `GET /api/screen/devices` shows each device's `health` with the `clock_drift` and `shared_token` flags and `state_lag`: the `state_version` of the zone the device shows (its own or its mirror target's) minus the one its last heartbeat reported.
- **Moving** (`src/registry.ts`, `src/moving.ts`): the superadmin's registry answers only on `CANCCU_SUPERADMIN_HOSTNAME`, and there only to a session admin marks `superadmin`; elsewhere it is 404 before any sign-in check, and on the platform hostnames every other route is 404. A move runs in one transaction that binds the old event and then the new one in turn (`asTenant`): the old side unassigns the device, its mirrors and its zone's sound (`detachDevice`), the new side gives it a place without a zone; the audit entry is a platform one. A moved kiosk's `GET /api/screen/device/home` answers the new event's screen URL; the current token also gets a transfer code (60 seconds, single use, in the new event's Valkey keys, made for that one token), which `POST /api/screen/device/transfer` swaps for a new token and cookie, keeping the replaced token as superseded for 7 days; a code whose device has moved on, been revoked or deleted since is `code_invalid`. Revoke, move, park and delete leave an event by one path (`src/detach.ts`): mirrors and sound go, the event hears what happened once (a device revoked earlier gets no `released` or second `revoked`), and it forgets the device's health; each event and the forget fail on their own, logged. A heartbeat re-reads the device's place after its writes and forgets them if the device has left meanwhile. A change of event is audited `screen.device.moved`, a new venue alone `screen.device.rehomed`, a PATCH that changes nothing writes nothing.
- **Zones:** an event gets its `main` zone once, on `tenants.tenant.created` or its first zone, assignment or pairing request, whichever comes first; a deleted `main` is not made again. `detachDevice` (`src/detach.ts`) is the one place that clears mirrors and sound devices when a device leaves the event.

- **Cookies** `canccu_pairing` and `canccu_device` are always `Secure`, as the contract says, so browsers keep them only over HTTPS: the IP-mode demo stack (plain http on port 80) cannot pair a real kiosk until TLS (#5). The tests send the cookies themselves.

## Soak and load

The stack tests in `test/stack/` (#310, step 3 plan O1–O3) run against a running stack, where a device stream may open: in the screen container, or a browser in its network namespace, since the venue IP check lets a screen in only from the venue's addresses (the loopback there). `scripts/demo-stack-test` runs them on the CI stack, which has two events ([docs/ci.md](../../docs/ci.md#demo-stack)): `push-latency.mjs`, `load.mjs` (100 screens over the two events) and the soak, accelerated. `measure.mjs` holds the budgets' arithmetic, tested in `test/stack-measure.test.ts`.

**The soak** (`scripts/screen-soak`, `test/stack/soak.mjs`) pairs a screen into the demo event's `main` zone and plays it in Chromium (the Playwright image) at 1920×1080 while the ticker changes, measuring the page's heap, DOM nodes and listeners after a forced GC at every hour of the page's clock. It fails when the heap grows more than the budget (8 MiB) between the first hours and the last, nodes or listeners double, the page throws, or the last change is not on the screen at the end; it writes `soak-report.md` and `samples.json`. With `--minutes M` a fake clock jumps a minute at a time, so 24 hours of rotation, heartbeats and stream reconnects run in M minutes (CI: 6).

**The real 24-hour run** is the lead's, on the test VM before step 3's exit. On the VM's stack (IP mode, so the demo event is `127.0.0.1`), with a session cookie that holds `screen.devices.pair` and `screen.ticker.write`:

```sh
CANCCU_DEMO_COMPOSE="docker compose -f deploy/compose.yml" CANCCU_DEMO_HOST=127.0.0.1 \
  DEMO_SESSION=<the canccu_session cookie> scripts/screen-soak --hours 24 --out soak-report
```

It changes the ticker every 5 minutes and ends by clearing it and revoking its screen; keep `soak-report/soak-report.md` with the step's exit notes.

With `CANCCU_VALKEY_URL` set, the render-queue tests also run the Lua scripts against that Valkey, in its database 15, which they empty. `test/render-smoke.mjs` renders a PDF through the screen container's own client and socket mount against the real renderer; CI's compose job runs it.

CI runs them against its own Postgres in the `module (screen)` job ([docs/ci.md](../../docs/ci.md#modules)).
